# Source evidence

Use while selecting sources and reconciling claims. Scale detail to the learning task; a short table is usually enough.

## Acquisition order

1. Identify the task's concepts and decisions; list what evidence would change the action.
2. Inspect supplied sources, dates, versions and relevant sections. Follow original references when accessible; mark unread originals rather than claiming to have checked them.
3. Fill decision-critical gaps with relevant primary records, standards/manuals, research or experienced practitioners' documented cases. Source type alone is not quality: assess methods, incentives, scope and recency.
4. Seek independent corroboration for consequential claims and a source/case that could contradict them. Two genuinely independent evidence lines are stronger than ten copies; absence of corroboration is a disclosed limit, not reason to manufacture consensus.
5. Stop at the agreed budget or sufficient task coverage. Note what is still unknown and the smallest useful next source.

## Compact evidence ledger

Record per decision-relevant claim, not every sentence:

| Field | What to capture |
|---|---|
| Claim / decision | Specific statement and task it informs |
| Kind | Direct observation, source-reported fact, practitioner heuristic, or curator inference |
| Locator | URL + section/page/date, or supplied file + location; authorized private-source alias when needed |
| Origin / independence | Original evidence versus derived retelling; shared datasets, incidents or organizational incentives |
| Applicability | Role, environment, version, population, stakes and prerequisites |
| Support / uncertainty | What supports it, what is unchecked, and confidence rationale in words |
| Challenge | Contradiction, counterexample, missing condition or planned falsifier |

Quotes and paraphrases stay attributed. Inferences state their supporting evidence and the logical gap; they are not observations. Preserve dated/versioned constraints, especially when technology, regulations or practice changed.

## Resolving disagreements

Ask whether speakers solved the same task under the same constraints. Compare incentives, available resources, skill level and failure costs before declaring a contradiction. If disagreement persists, keep alternatives visible and specify a discriminating check; do not average incompatible prescriptions or treat majority repetition as truth.

## Minimal sufficiency

Coverage means the scoped task's prerequisites, major choices and likely costly failures are addressed **or explicitly unresolved**. It does not mean mastery of the whole domain. Missing critical evidence prevents that recommendation; a provisional map may still help plan learning. No source access yields an acquisition plan only.
