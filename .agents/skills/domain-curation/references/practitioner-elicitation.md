# Practitioner elicitation

Use for an authorized interview, supplied discussion, or a public account of real practice. Interviews are optional; no permission to contact people or record them is implied. Ask consent and intended attribution before any authorized interview; redact confidential material and follow consent limits for storage/sharing.

## Anchor in an actual incident

Ask for a recent concrete task and reconstruct its timeline. Avoid generic “best practices” prompts alone:

1. What outcome were you responsible for, and under what constraints?
2. At which moment did you need to choose? What did you notice first?
3. Which cue would a novice miss? How did you distinguish signal from noise?
4. Which alternatives did you reject, and what trade-off mattered?
5. What failed or nearly failed? How did you detect and recover?
6. When would your approach be wrong? Give a contrasting case.
7. What prerequisites or resources did your method silently depend on?
8. What would you teach a newcomer to try safely, and what should they not attempt alone?

Use short follow-up probes (“What changed your mind?”, “What would make you switch?”). Ask for an example where applicable, but never pressure someone to reveal protected information. Repeated observations from the same practitioner are valuable detail, not independent corroboration.

## Conditional judgment card

For each actionable insight capture:

- **Decision:** task and choice the card helps with.
- **When:** triggering cues and context/prerequisites.
- **Prefer:** action and reason/trade-off; alternatives considered.
- **Unless:** exceptions, contraindications, novice limits and escalation cues.
- **Check:** observable result, failure signal and recovery/stop action.
- **Evidence:** incident/source locator, attributed heuristic versus inference, corroboration, disagreement and uncertainty.

One paragraph may suffice. Do not invent missing fields: mark them unknown and attach a follow-up question. Where feasible and authorized, invite the practitioner to correct your paraphrase; this establishes fidelity of interpretation, not universal validity.

## No practitioner access

Extract the same fields from accessible documented cases, label them secondary accounts, and retain context gaps. If no such evidence exists, deliver foundational knowledge and an interview/question backlog; tacit judgment remains unverified. Never simulate an expert persona and report the result as practitioner testimony.

## Synthetic example — format only

Task: choose how to investigate an unfamiliar local tool failure. Cue: failure began immediately after a configuration edit. Prefer: inspect the changed configuration before broader environment changes. Unless: evidence points elsewhere or inspection risks exposing secrets. Check: compare a safe minimal reproduction with expected behavior; stop before modifying an unrelated user service. Evidence: **invented teaching example, not veteran testimony or a validated domain recommendation**.
